Does Market News Help a Zero-Shot LLM Fill the New-Asset Slots? A Controlled Test on FAR-Trans

Vladislav Kutranov · Team Sparse Signals (Zlata Kranina, Maria Kodzaeva) · LLM4Rec, HSE, Homework 3
Repository: github.com/elebrane/ML-RS-Project-vladislav-kutranov, folder week9/news_rag · experiment commit: [add after push]
Abstract. In FAR-Trans most purchases are repeats, and the course baselines show that recency is hard to beat. Our HW2 hybrid ("Duet") found that an LLM which only restates code-computed facts adds nothing. In HW3 we narrow the LLM to the one place where transaction data have no signal beyond popularity, the slots for never-bought assets below the client's own assets, and give it evidence from outside the dataset: a time-safe daily summary of the Greek press coverage of each candidate stock (GDELT), with masked headlines. Development results (1 May 2021 cutoff, 1,498 warm clients): For 669 Stock-main FAR-Trans clients (May 2021), a zero-shot Qwen3-4B ranker without news changed mean nDCG@10 over all 1,498 clients by -0.0061 [-0.0087, -0.0037] relative to the popularity order of the same 20 candidates. On the first 174 clients of the cohort order (78 LLM calls; the M run was stopped at the deadline), adding news changed nDCG@10 by +0.0036 [-0.0001, +0.0096] relative to the same ranker without news (3 clients better, 1 worse) and by -0.0049 [-0.0122, +0.0009] relative to the popularity order. All results are development results, not official benchmark scores.

1. Introduction

RQ. For warm FAR-Trans clients whose main asset category is Stock and who have free new-asset slots (669 of 1,498 development clients at the 1 May 2021 cutoff), does retrieving time-safe Greek-press news evidence for each candidate stock — article counts over 30 and 7 days, attention relative to the stock's usual level, average tone and the two latest masked headlines, all first seen by GDELT before the cutoff — change nDCG@10 of a zero-shot Qwen3-4B-Instruct ranker relative to the same ranker without news, when the candidates, their initial order and the client's own assets are held fixed?

Why this place. Before any LLM run we checked, without a model, every transaction signal we could build for ordering never-bought assets: co-buyers of the client's assets, "bought X then Y" transitions, 7- and 90-day popularity, buying spikes, name and fund-house similarity, subcategory. On March–April 2021 none beat 30-day popularity (0.548–0.573 vs 0.571 nDCG@10 over buyers; perfect order 0.601). Of 160 new purchases in the April block, 156 were stocks of large Greek companies (Piraeus, Alpha, NBG, OTE, Mytilineos) — firms that the press covers daily. Retail investors are known to buy attention-grabbing stocks [5], so outside news is the one candidate signal left. LLM role: Ranker only. It changes only the order (and choice) of never-bought stocks in the free slots; the own-asset block and the candidate set come from the HW2 rule cascade and are identical in every arm.

3. Method

List assembly (all arms). (1) Up to 10 own assets in the HW2 rule order (decayed purchase frequency; this is "Duet" cell 1 — the Duet v2 encoder gave the same 10-item set as the rule for every client, so it is not used). (2) Free slots k = 10 − own assets. (3) A block of the 20 most-bought never-traded assets of the client's main category (bank buyers in the 30 days before the cutoff). C0 fills the k slots in this popularity order; A1 and M let the LLM choose k of the 20. The LLM is called only for Stock-main clients (the news layer covers listed stocks; the rule reads past data only); other clients keep C0.

Prompt. Client: main category, number of assets, buys in the last and previous 90 days, sectors of past buys, profile. 20 candidate rows in random order with random IDs (A10–A99): category, sector, bank buyers in 30 days and rank, 30-day price change; no names or ISINs. M adds, under each stock with collected news, one line of numbers (articles in 30 and 7 days, ratio to the stock's own level over the previous 60 days, share of coverage, average GDELT tone) and the two latest headlines that name the company, with the name replaced by "[company]". A1 is the identical prompt without these lines (a unit test checks this). Output: a JSON array of k IDs. Invalid answers keep their valid IDs and are completed in the initial order (repair); with no valid ID the list falls back to C0; every case is counted.

Time safety. Transactions, prices and profiles strictly before the cutoff (HW2 assertion). News counts use UTC days up to cutoff − 2; a headline is used only if GDELT first saw it before 21:00 UTC on cutoff − 1 (Athens midnight); both are asserted and unit-tested. Model. Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 @ cdbee75, greedy, thinking off, float16 on Apple M3 Pro (MPS, 18 GB), torch 2.11.0, transformers 4.57.6, 90 new tokens. Code: block_ranker.py, news.py, collect_gdelt.py, 6 unit tests.

Observation. Without news, the LLM keeps only about half of C0's picks, chooses less popular stocks and stocks that rose more in the last 30 days, and is not biased towards the first rows shown. It removed 33 assets that clients then bought and brought in 4. Possible explanation (not tested): the model reads "price change" as a reason to buy, while in this bank the crowd signal (how many clients bought it) is what predicts the next first purchase. With news (M, first 78 clients): the picks barely move — overlap with C0 0.478 vs 0.451 without news, median popularity rank 8 vs 10, the same preference for recent price gains; it drops 5 bought assets instead of 6. News nudges the model slightly back towards popular stocks but does not change what drives its choice.

Limitations. One development month and one cutoff; the effect can only appear for the ~80 clients who bought something from their block (April estimate). Partial news coverage, no large banks. GDELT tone is lexicon-based on machine translation; title matching is precise but misses articles that do not name the company in the title. Not run for lack of time: the placebo with news moved to other stocks (A3), numbers without headlines (A2), a second shown order and visible names; these are implemented (run_hw3_night2.command). Purchase prediction is not suitability or profit; not investment advice.
